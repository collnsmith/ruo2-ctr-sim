SLIDE OUTLINE: potential response of the surface of RuO2(110) films on TiO2(110), 3.3 / 4.1 / 7 nm
=========================================================================================================
Main message: under the same protocol, at the same surface-sensitive reflections, the 3.3 and 7 nm films respond
strongly and reversibly to potential, while the 4.1 nm film does not respond at all. The comparison uses relative
intensities only. No absolute coverage is claimed.

Figures are in analysis/2026-11-06_stationary_cv/ unless another folder is named.
[NEEDED] marks something that must be in hand before presenting.
Potentials are vs Ag/AgCl. RHE = Ag/AgCl + 0.26 V in 0.1 M H2SO4.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 1  Title
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Proposed title: "A 4.1 nm RuO2(110) film whose surface does not respond to potential:
  in situ surface X-ray diffraction of 3.3, 4.1 and 7 nm films"
- Names, beamtime, date.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 2  Question
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Does the surface of epitaxial RuO2(110) films respond to potential in the same way at different thicknesses?
- Approach: watch surface-sensitive diffraction intensity while the potential changes, and compare the films
  at the same reflections.
- Why relative: absolute OH coverage from CTR fits depends on model assumptions (the bridging-O height).
  A change, or no change, at a fixed reflection does not.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 3  Samples and measurements
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Films:
  - 3.3 nm: CVs only.
  - 4.1 nm: fully strained (Jeong et al.).
  - 7 nm: partly relaxed.
- Electrolyte 0.1 M H2SO4, Ag/AgCl reference.
- CTRs at -0.238, -0.1, 0, 0.15, 0.35, 0.7 V and OCV (4.1 and 7 nm).
- Stationary CVs at a fixed HKL:
  - 30 s at 0.0 V, then cycles 0 to 0.7 V at 5 mV/s (280 s per cycle);
  - 14 scans: 4 on 3.3 nm, 5 on 4.1 nm, 5 on 7 nm (one of them the control rod).
- Potential table (small):

  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

SLIDE 4  Why these reflections
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- On the (0 +-1) rods (H + K odd) the metal contributions cancel, so the oxygen and adsorbate layers dominate:
  these rods are the surface-sensitive ones.
- Even rods and (2 0) are insensitive to the surface species: they serve as controls.
- Fingerprint: a surface change moves L 2.75 and L 3.16 / 3.25 in OPPOSITE directions (down vs up).
  A beam, absorption or normalization artefact would move every point the same way.
- Figure: ../2026-11-05_oh_fits/fig2_oh_sensitivity_01.png (sensitivity along (0 1)), the CV points marked.
- Speaker note: the model is used here only to choose the reflections and predict signs, not to fit.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 5  Raw data: same reflection, two films
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: fig_raw.png (7 nm scan 314 vs 4.1 nm scan 498, both at (0 +-1 3.16)).
- 7 nm: the intensity follows every cycle, +15 to +20%. 4.1 nm: flat within noise.
- The grey Ti ROI is an internal reference: the detector peak splits slightly into a "Ru" and a "Ti" spot.
  The response is in the Ru spot. The Ti spot stays nearly flat (in 314 it tracks by about 2%).
- Speaker note: the slow +3% rise over 200 images in 498 is common to both ROIs, so it is beam or normalization
  drift (these scans have no monitor). It is not locked to the potential.

SLIDE 6  All CV scans
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: fig_cv.png (cycle-averaged change vs E, anodic and cathodic, per scan; rows 4.1 / 3.3 / 7 nm).
- 3.3 and 7 nm:

- 4.1 nm: flat at all four reflections (2.75 twice, 2.84, 3.16, 3.25).

  | 3.3 nm | -13 | | +13 | -11 |
  | 7 nm | -25, -38 | | +19 | +12 (noisy) |

- Speaker notes:

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 7  KEY RESULT: the response, with no timing or model assumption
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: fig_robust.png, panel a.
- Method: the best sine wave in each scan, with the period (60 to 130 images) and the phase left free, plus a drift term.
- Peak-to-peak oscillation of Ru+Ti:
  - 4.1 nm: 1.0 to 1.9% (5 scans, 4 reflections);
  - 3.3 nm: 9.6 to 11.1% (3 scans);
  - 7 nm: 9.1 to 36.7% (4 scans);
  - 7 nm (2 0 1.75) control: 0.2%.
- The Ru spot alone gives 1.1 to 2.4% (4.1 nm) against 13 to 42% (3.3 and 7 nm).
- Bottom line: 5 to 35 times smaller on 4.1 nm at the same reflections.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 8  What it is not (controls)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each objection and its answer:

- **Timing or analysis choice:** the amplitude is found with period and phase free; the flat scans stay
  at or below 2.8% for any timing.
- **Beam, absorption, electrolyte thickness, bubbles:** these would move the Ti ROI and the control rod too.
  Both stay flat, and the response is locked to the potential cycle and has opposite signs at different L.
- **Detector saturation or poor statistics:** the 4.1 nm CA levels run from 4k to 129k counts. The low-count
  4.1 nm scans (496: 8k, 501: 4k) match the responding 7 nm scans (4k to 5k) and are just as flat.
- **The 4.1 nm points happen to be insensitive:** in the model, a surface change of the size seen on 7 nm moves
  these points by 14 to 25% for any 4.1 nm thickness from 10 to 14.5 TL and any bridging-O height. Without the model,
  the 3.3 and 7 nm films bracket 4.1 nm in thickness and both respond at these reflections.
- **The potential was not applied:** [NEEDED] the EC-Lab current for scans 496-503 next to a 7 nm CV.
  This is the most important missing control. Put the current traces on this slide.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 9  CTR series: consistent, secondary
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: fig_robust.png, panel b.
- Method: each film's OH rod at each potential divided by the same film's 0 V rod, with one free scale and
  the TiO2 Bragg peak left out. The measure is the rms of ln(ratio) over the whole rod.
- Results:
  - repeatability (even rods, 7 nm OCV repeat): 0.016 to 0.031;
  - 4.1 nm: 0.032 to 0.046 at every controlled potential, so no change from -0.238 to 0.7 V;
  - 7 nm: 0.028 to 0.034 up to 0.15 V, then 0.056 at 0.35 V and 0.064 at 0.7 V, so it changes above 0.15 V.
- Speaker notes:
  - This is 2 to 3 times the repeatability: real but much weaker evidence than the CVs (slide 7).
  - The 7 nm CTR change is about a third of the CV change at the same L. Possible reasons:
    - the hold time at each potential for a CTR is long, while the CV sweeps;
    - the response may grow with cycling (7 nm repeat at L 2.75: 21% in scan 311, 37% in the later scan 344).
  - Differences between potentials in a CTR series could also be drift in time. Use the [NEEDED] timeline.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 10  Where the 7 nm surface changes
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Read from the curves (fig_cv.png): half of the change is reached at about 0.2-0.3 V Ag/AgCl (about 0.5 V RHE),
  over about 0.1-0.15 V, with no hysteresis at 5 mV/s.
- 4.1 nm: nothing from 0 to 0.7 V Ag/AgCl (0.26 to 0.96 V RHE), and nothing in its CTRs down to -0.238 V (0.02 V RHE).
- CTR rods at 0.35 and 0.7 V: the 4.1 and 7 nm films give the same OH-rod fits (within errors), while at -0.1 and 0 V
  they differ. So 4.1 nm sits at all potentials in the state 7 nm reaches only above its step.
  This comes from the OH fits of the CTRs and is model-based; [CHECK] present it as supporting only.
- Speaker note: as the 7 nm step's potential, quote the half-change point read from the curves rather than the
  fit value (model fit E0 = 0.22-0.27 V Ag/AgCl).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 10b  Comparison with the work function of these films (Jeong et al., Nat. Commun. 17, 2516 (2026); arXiv 2507.07575)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Their result: KPFM work function of RuO2/TiO2(110) vs thickness is NON-MONOTONIC, peaking at t_c of about 4 nm,
  more than 1 eV (about 1.15 eV) above relaxed films.
- Their structure at t_c: below it, strained and polar (SHG mm2, ptychography shows polar displacements at the interface);
  above it, relaxing (RSM: onset between 4 and 6.5 nm) and nonpolar (4/mmm).
- Ours: the one film that does not respond (4.1 nm) is the one at the work-function peak; 3.3 nm (below the peak)
  and 7 nm (relaxed, low work function) respond.
- Point: two independent probes, both non-monotonic in thickness, with the extreme at the same thickness.
- [NEEDED] the actual work function at 3.3, 4.1 and 7 nm, ideally on these same samples. The link holds only if
  3.3 nm is clearly below the 4.1 nm value. The thicknesses are about 0.8 nm apart, so the thickness calibration matters.
- Caveats for the speaker:
  - KPFM is measured in air or vacuum; in electrolyte the water layer and adsorbates screen part of the difference.
  - The work function alone does not give the direction of the shift in OH binding. The simple argument
    (higher work function, so OH binds more weakly and forms only at more positive potential) would hold the
    4.1 nm surface in the WATER state across the window.
  - Our CTR fits cannot detect a polar distortion through the whole film (4.1 nm: 0.00 +- 0.045 Å, polar_test.py),
    so the structural link stays at the interface.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 11  Interpretation (labelled as such)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The 4.1 nm surface is held in one state across the window, the same state 7 nm reaches at high potential.
- Candidate explanations, with what argues for and against each:
  - **Strain or polarization at the work-function peak** (4.1 nm sits at t_c, the work-function maximum): strain alone
    does not fit (3.3 nm is at least as strained and responds), but the work-function peak is non-monotonic, like
    our data. This depends on the [NEEDED] work function values.
  - **Sample history** (the first polarization, beam dose, preparation): the 4.1 nm OCV rod differs from every
    controlled potential. Testable on a fresh spot or a fresh sample.
  - **A blocking adsorbate** (e.g. sulfate) on this film only: testable in HClO4.
- Say plainly: one sample per thickness, and the thickness trend is not monotonic.

SLIDE 13  Summary
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- At the same surface-sensitive reflections and with the same protocol:
  - 3.3 and 7 nm: 9 to 37% reversible oscillation with potential, with the opposite-sign fingerprint of a surface change;
  - 4.1 nm: under 2%, at 4 reflections in 5 scans;
  - OH-insensitive control: 0.2%.
- The CTR series agrees: 7 nm changes above 0.15 V Ag/AgCl, 4.1 nm does not change from -0.238 to 0.7 V.
- The 4.1 nm surface does not respond to potential. Why is open: strain alone does not explain it.


=============================================================================================================
BACKUP SLIDES
=============================================================================================================

B1  CV processing
- ROI sums per image. No SPEC file, so there is no monitor or filter normalization and time comes from the image number.
- The period in images is found from the data (dominant period) and snapped to whole or half cycles after the 30 s hold.
  The timing shift (within 0.15 of a period) is the one that correlates best with E, which fixes the sign.
- Each signal is relative to the 30 s hold at 0 V, averaged over the cycles in 14 E bins per sweep.
- Scans 147 and 222: see slide 6 notes.
- Files: extract.py, extract2.py, extract3.py, robust_cv.py.

B2  The Ru and Ti ROIs
- The detector peak splits slightly; in the CTRs one ROI covered both. Ru+Ti is therefore the quantity comparable with the CTRs.
- The response sits in the Ru spot. The Ti spot is flat except in 344 (noisy; Ru/Ti 6.2 vs 2.1 in 311 at the same
  reflection, so the ROIs were drawn differently).
- The Ru/Ti count ratio depends on where the ROIs were drawn, so it is not a measure of film vs substrate.
- Open question: is part of the substrate signal incoherent with the film and surface? The flat Ti spot suggests it is.
  This affects CTR modelling (absolute values) but not this relative comparison.

B3  Model-based fits (only if asked)
- Joint fit of each film's CVs with an OH fraction stepping with E. For 7 nm, red. chi2 4.7 (Ru+Ti, coherent model),
  step at 0.22 V Ag/AgCl. The OH fraction and the bridging-O height are strongly correlated (errors +-0.5 to 0.9),
  so the step cannot be assigned to water -> OH on Ru-CUS rather than bridging-O deprotonation.
- For 4.1 nm, the OH fraction changes by at most 0.03 (coherent model) to 0.10 (film spot only) across the window.
- CTR OH fits under three bridging-O assumptions: the absolute OH fraction depends on the assumption, but the film
  difference at -0.1 and 0 V holds in all three (../2026-11-05_oh_fits/summary.pdf).

B4  Why no single-point ratio from the CTRs
- I(3.16)/I(2.75) relative to 0 V depends on the chosen L window and a +-0.003 L alignment shift
  (10-90% range 0.8 to 1.4 for both films). The whole-rod comparison (slide 9) is used instead.

B5  Measurement notes
- The 7 nm (2 0) and (0 2) CTRs at 0 and 0.7 V used a 0.6 degree incidence angle (others 0.5). This changes the footprint,
  which each rod's own scale absorbs, plus under 1%. These rods are not used for the surface comparison.
- 4.1 nm scans 496 and 497 (same reflection, back to back) differ 5x in counts (filter or count time). Relative changes are unaffected.


=============================================================================================================
LIKELY QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
=============================================================================================================
Q: Was the potential applied during the 4.1 nm CVs?
A: [NEEDED] EC current for scans 496-503. Without it this is the weakest point of the talk.

Q: Is it a thickness effect?
A: Not shown. 3.3 nm responds, 4.1 nm does not, 7 nm responds, with one sample each. We claim that this 4.1 nm film
   differs, not that there is a thickness trend.

Q: Could the 4.1 nm points be at fringe positions where the surface contributes little?
A: No. Four different L values all stay flat, and the model gives 14-25% sensitivity at each one for any plausible thickness.

Q: Beam damage or drift?
A: The CV response is periodic and locked to the potential; drift cannot produce it, and the 4.1 nm drift (+3% over
   200 images) is common to both ROIs. For the CTR series: [NEEDED] the timeline.

Q: Why is the 7 nm change smaller in the CTR series than in the CVs?
A: Long holds vs sweeps, or a response that grows with cycling (311 -> 344). Open.

Q: How much OH?
A: Not claimed; it depends on the bridging-O assumption (backup B3). The conclusion rests on relative changes.

Q: Is 0.1 M H2SO4 a problem (sulfate)?
A: Possibly, for the 4.1 nm film. A test in HClO4 is on the next-steps slide.
