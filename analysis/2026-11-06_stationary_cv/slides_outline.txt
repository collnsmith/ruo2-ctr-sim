SLIDE OUTLINE: the potential response of the surface of RuO2(110) films on TiO2(110), 3.3 / 4.1 / 7 nm
=========================================================================================================

THE ARGUMENT, IN ONE SENTENCE
At the same surface-sensitive reflections and under the same potential program, the 3.3 and 7 nm films switch reversibly
between two surface states near 0.2-0.25 V vs Ag/AgCl, while the 4.1 nm film does not change at all from -0.24 to 0.7 V.
The CTRs, fitted with Rao et al.'s bridging-O values and with an alternative, put the 4.1 nm surface in the state the
7 nm surface reaches only at high potential.

ASSUMPTION, STATED ON THE SLIDES (slides 2, 6 and 14; backup B8)
The 4.1 nm film is taken to have been under potential control on day 4. The potentiostat recorded zero current in every
day-4 file (no charge per cycle, no charging at potential steps), so the current record cannot confirm it. What supports it:
  - Ewe followed the program in every file, with the same noise as on the other days.
  - The rest potential was real: stable at 0.37-0.40 V, and it jumped when the beam hit the film. A disconnected working
    electrode on this BioLogic reads about 10 V.
  - The CE and RE sat in the same reservoir as the film, and the cell had no bubbles (inspected after the run).
  - Resistances between every pair of electrodes through the cell, and from each clip to its electrode, read a few hundred
    ohms, checked several times during the run. A blocked film would read open.
With an unbroken electrolyte and a wetted, conducting film, Ewe can only follow the program if the film's potential does.
So the charge flowed and was not recorded. The cause is not identified; most likely the current returned around the ammeter.

HOW THE EVIDENCE IS ORDERED (strongest first; each step stands without the ones after it)
  1. CVs, no model, at the potential EC-Lab applied (timing from the instrument clocks): 4.1 nm flat, 3.3 and 7 nm switch
     (slides 5-7).
  2. CTR rods, no model: 4.1 nm unchanged across potentials, 7 nm changes above 0.15 V (slide 8).
  3. CTR fits, two bridging-O assumptions including Rao's: the films differ at <= 0 V and agree at >= 0.35 V (slide 9).
  4. CTR -> CV: the surface states fitted to the CTRs, used with nothing adjusted, reproduce the 7 nm CVs up to 0.35 V and
     are consistent with the flat 4.1 nm CVs. A fit of only an OH step to the CVs, with the CTR structure and Rao's
     bridging O, gives the 7 nm onset and bounds the 4.1 nm change (slides 10-11).

WHAT WE DO NOT CLAIM
  - An absolute OH coverage (it depends on the bridging-O assumption).
  - A thickness trend (one sample per thickness; 3.3 nm responds, 4.1 nm does not).
  - A mechanism.

SLIDE 4  Why these reflections
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- On the (0 +-1) rods (H + K odd) the metal contributions cancel, so the oxygen and adsorbate layers dominate:
  these rods are the surface-sensitive ones. Even rods and (2 0) are insensitive to the surface species: controls.
- Fingerprint of a surface change: L 2.75 and L 3.16 move in OPPOSITE directions. A beam, absorption or normalization
  artefact moves every point the same way.

SLIDE 6  KEY RESULT 1: the CV response at the potential EC-Lab applied
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: slide06b_cv_response_synced.png (main). slide06_cv_amplitudes.png gives the same picture with period and phase
  left free (no timing input at all).
- Method:
  - Every X-ray point gets the potential EC-Lab applied at its SPEC time. The instrument clocks agree: the logged Start
    marks fall 1-2 s from the EC-Lab start on every day, day 4 included.
  - Per scan, I/I0 = slow drift + A x (a step at 0.23 V), so A is the change from 0 to 0.7 V. Nothing is fitted to the
    timing.
  - The error of A is the larger of a noise-surrogate error (the residual phase-randomised, same noise spectrum) and an
    autocorrelation-robust (Newey-West) error.
- Change from 0 to 0.7 V, weighted per reflection:

  | Reflection | 3.3 nm | 7 nm | 4.1 nm |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | (0 ±1 2.75) | -9.7 ± 1.3% | -20.3 ± 0.7% | +0.3 ± 0.3% |
  | (0 ±1 3.16) | +9.3 ± 0.5% | +14.9 ± 0.9% | -0.3 ± 0.7% |
  | (0 ±1 3.25) | -8.5 ± 0.8% | +8.3 ± 0.8% | +2.6 ± 1.0% |

  4.1 nm (0 -1 2.84): -0.2 ± 0.3%. 4.1 nm control (-2 0 1.75): +0.04 ± 0.20%.
- The 4.1 nm response is at most 3% and 12% of the 3.3 nm response, and 1% and 8% of the 7 nm response, at 2.75 and 3.16
  (2 sigma).
- On the slide: "4.1 nm: potential control assumed; the current record was lost on day 4 (backup B8)".
- Speaker note: by eye, 498 and 503 show about 1% of structure (498: the anodic sweep about 1% above the cathodic below
  0.35 V; 503: both sweeps about 0.5-0.9% lower at 0.5-0.7 V). A shape-free test against noise surrogates finds no
  potential-locked response in any 4.1 nm scan or ROI (498 p = 0.77, 503 p = 0.26, 519 p = 0.76; all six scans combined
  p = 0.56-0.66 per ROI; weak41.py, fig_weak41.png). The same test picks up 222, 314 and 125.
- Speaker note: 501 at 3.25 (+2.6%) is not a clean step. Binned by potential, its signal does not stay up above 0.4 V,
  unlike 316 and 125, so it is not counted as a response.
- The same with period and phase left free (slide06_cv_amplitudes.png), peak-to-peak oscillation of Ru+Ti:
  - 4.1 nm: 1.0 to 1.9% (5 scans, 4 reflections);
  - 3.3 nm: 9.6 to 12.5% (223, a repeat of 222 at 3.16, gives 12.5%: +15.4 +- 0.6% from 0 to 0.7 V, against +12.7%);
  - 7 nm: 9.1 to 36.7%;
  - 7 nm (0 1 2.75) four times in time order (311, 341, 343, 344): Ru spot 30, 42, 42, 42%. The sum (21-37%) depends on
    how much Ti the ROIs held (Ru/Ti 1.6-6.2). So the response reproduces and does not grow with cycling after the first scan;
    the 0.6 degree incidence of 344 does not change it.
  - OH-blind controls (triangles), each at its own point-noise level:
    - 7 nm (2 0 1.75): 0.2%;
    - 4.1 nm (-2 0 1.75): 0.8% (noise 1.1%);
    - 3.3 nm (-2 0 1.75), (-2 0 1), (-1 0 2.32), (1 0 1.75): 2.3, 3.4, 3.5, 1.1% (noise 2.4, 4.0, 3.7, 2.7%).
- What it is not:
  - Timing or analysis choice: period and phase are free; the 4.1 nm scans stay at or below 2.8% for any timing.
  - Beam, absorption or electrolyte: these would move the Ti ROI and the control rod too, and the signal would not change
    sign with L.
  - Saturation or statistics: the low-count 4.1 nm scans (4-8k counts) are as flat as the high-count ones and at the same
    level as responding 7 nm scans.
  - Insensitive points: in the model, a 7 nm-sized change moves the 4.1 nm points by 14-25% for any thickness from 10 to 14.5 TL
    and any bridging-O height. Without the model, 3.3 and 7 nm bracket 4.1 nm and respond at the same reflections.
  - Beam dose before the CVs: the 4.1 nm CVs were run on a FRESH SPOT after its CTR series (logbook).
  - Surface specificity on a responding film, the same day and cell: on 3.3 nm,
    - (-2 0 1.75) oscillates by 2.3% peak-to-peak (point noise 2.4%);
    - (-2 0 1) at 25 mV/s by 3.4% (point noise 4%);
    - (-1 0 2.32) by 3.5% (point noise 3.7%);
    - (1 0 1.75) by 1.1% (point noise 2.7%), an odd rod the model says is OH-blind;
    - (0 -1) by 10-15%, at 2.75, 3.16 (twice) and 3.25.
    The model predicts exactly this: at most 0.4% at (-2 0), 2% at (-1 0 2.32) and 3% at (1 0 1.75), against 17-51% on
    (0 -1). The (1 0 1.75) case matters most: it is also an odd rod, so the response is not just 'odd vs even'. It is the
    reflections the model picks out as OH-sensitive.
    Speaker note: in 135 the two sub-spots of the split peak each wander by 3-5% (near noise), roughly in opposite phase,
    and cancel in the full signal. At most a small shift between the spots; no change in total intensity.
  - The potential not applied: for 4.1 nm this is the stated assumption (top of this outline; backup B8).

SLIDE 7  The switch: reversible, at the same potential in both responding films
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: slide07_cv_curves.png (cycle-averaged change vs E, both sweeps, every OH-sensitive scan; 147 as its Ru spot).
- 3.3 and 7 nm:
  - the fingerprint (down at 2.75, up at 3.16);
  - the anodic and cathodic sweeps overlap, so the switch is reversible with no hysteresis at 5 mV/s;
  - half of the change is reached at 0.20-0.31 V (3.3 nm; averaged curve 0.25 V) and 0.17-0.24 V (7 nm; averaged curve
    0.22 V) vs Ag/AgCl with the clock-synced potential, the same within the scan spread, about 0.45-0.5 V vs RHE.
    The earlier timing gave 0.24 and 0.21 V.
- 4.1 nm: flat at all four reflections.
- Speaker notes:
  - 3.3 nm at L 3.25 moves the other way from 7 nm. It is not interpreted:
    - CV 125 was measured with an older orientation matrix, at about half the rod's intensity at that L, so probably
      off the rod maximum;
    - 3.25 sits on the steep edge of the 3.3 nm rod (backup B10);
    - the CTR model gives that sign only for a few sharp-fringe film structures.
  - Scan 147 (0 -1 1.25) is left out of the fits because it is hysteretic. With the hold at the logged Start (image 50) and
    the period from its Ru peaks (72 images), the Ru spot drops on the first anodic sweep with no lag, then stays 20-34% low.
    It recovers only partly (to -12%) at 0.3-0.4 V on later anodic sweeps, about 80 s after the potential returns to 0 V.
    It is the only scan with a lag, at the reflection the model calls most OH-sensitive (-38 to -51%). It is shown as an
    open point, not used for or against the main result. Ru is only 15% of the summed signal there, so the sum barely moves.
  - Scan 222 is used only up to its Stop (point 250), before the switch to 25 mV/s.

SLIDE 8  KEY RESULT 2: the CTR rods themselves, in measurement order (no model)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: slide08_ctr_rods.png (a, b: ratios of two rods along L; c: the rms summary).
- Method: one OH rod divided by another, with one free scale and the TiO2 Bragg peak left out. The measure is the
  rms of ln(ratio) over the rod.
- 7 nm (0 1):
  - Repeat measurements at the same potential, hours apart (0.7 V: 325/326 vs 339/340; -0.1 V: 333/334 vs 337/338):
    0.039 and 0.040. This is the repeatability.
  - 0.7 V vs -0.1 V: 0.077 on the way down (325/326 -> 333/334) and 0.078 on the way back up (337/338 -> 339/340,
    measured back to back).
  - So the change is twice the repeatability, appears in both directions, and returns: it follows the potential, not
    time.
- 4.1 nm (0 -1), measured 0 -> -0.1 -> 0.15 -> 0.7 -> -0.238 -> 0.35 V:
  - every back-to-back pair is 0.031-0.043, at the repeatability level;
  - this includes 0.7 V -> -0.238 V, the largest step (0.039);
  - the order jumps up and down in potential, so a slow drift cannot hide a change.
- Speaker notes:
  - The single steps in the 7 nm descending series (0.7 -> 0.35 -> 0.15 -> 0 -> -0.1 V) are each 0.019-0.033;
    the change builds up across the series.
  - The 4.1 nm OCV rod was measured LAST, on the fresh CV spot, so its difference from the others (0.11) mixes
    spot and condition. It is not used.

SLIDE 9  KEY RESULT 3: CTR fits with Rao's bridging-O values, and with an alternative
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: slide09_oh_fraction.png (a: Rao; b: 0 Å; c: the film difference under both).
- Method:
  - Film structure: fitted on the well-behaved rods (0 +-1), (-1 0), (-2 0).
  - OH fraction: fitted on the (0 +-1) rods per film and potential, each rod file with its own scale.
  - The bridging-O height cannot be fitted together with the OH fraction (they trade off), so it is FIXED by assumption:
    - (a) Rao et al. 2017 (single crystal, potentials converted with the logbook's -0.238 V = 0 V RHE): 0.35 Å up to
      0.35 V (0.59 V RHE, just below Rao's 0.6 V boundary), 0 Å at 0.7 V (0.94 V RHE);
    - (b) 0 Å at every potential.
  - Both fit equally well (red. chi2 14.8 and 14.5).
- Result, the same under both: 4.1 nm minus 7 nm OH fraction:

  | Assumption | -0.1 V | 0 V | 0.15 V | 0.35 V | 0.7 V |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | (a) Rao | +0.50 | +0.40 | +0.15 | -0.05 | +0.10 |
  | (b) 0 Å | +0.50 | +0.50 | +0.25 | +0.05 | +0.10 |

  The films differ at or below 0 V and agree at 0.35 and 0.7 V. The absolute values move with the assumption; the
  difference does not.
- Say plainly:
  - At each potential alone the difference is at about the 2-sigma level; it is the same sign at -0.1 and 0 V and under both
    assumptions.
  - A third fit with the bridging O left free (backup B3, slideB3_bridging_O_free.png) gives the same best-fit pattern but constrains
    almost nothing (ranges 0 to 1). Show it as the reason the height has to be fixed, not as support.
  - The fall at 0.7 V under Rao's values comes from the imposed bridging-O change (0.35 -> 0 Å). It is not a measured change.

SLIDE 10  CTR -> CV: the CTR-fitted surface states, used unchanged, against the CVs
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: slide10_ctr_to_cv.png (Rao and 0 Å states).
- Method: the surface state at each CTR potential (OH fraction from slide 9, bridging O as assumed, film structure from the
  CTR fit) gives a predicted intensity at each CV reflection, relative to the 0 V state. Nothing is adjusted to the CVs.
- 7 nm, predicted vs measured, up to 0.35 V:

  | Reflection | 0.15 V predicted | 0.15 V measured | 0.35 V predicted | 0.35 V measured |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | (0 1 2.75) | -7 to -10% | -9% | -10 to -15% | -24% |
  | (0 1 3.16) | +7 to +10% | +7% | +10 to +15% | +14% |

  So the sign, the onset and roughly the size are predicted.
- 4.1 nm: the CTR states predict changes within about +-10% of zero, with uncertainty bars that include zero. The CVs are
  flat (within +-1.5%).
- Say plainly:
  - The CTR states carry +-15-25% uncertainty at a single reflection; the CVs are about ten times more precise. The CTRs
    show consistency, and the CVs give the sharp result.
  - At 0.7 V the CTR states predict a partial return for 7 nm (2.75: -5 to -10%) that the CVs do not show (-27%).
  - (0 1 3.25) is overpredicted; with the clock-synced timing 316 gives +8 ± 2% from 0 to 0.7 V.
  - Rao's bridging-O change at 0.7 V predicts a +17% rise at (0 -1 2.75) on 4.1 nm (range +1 to +33%). The flat CV argues
    that this change does not happen on the 4.1 nm film.

SLIDE 11  CTR structure + Rao's bridging O, fitted to the CVs
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: slide11_cv_fit.png (a: where 7 nm switches; b: 7 nm CVs with the fitted step; c: 4.1 nm CVs against the same step).
- Method: film structure and water height from the CTR fits, bridging O at Rao's low-potential value (0.35 Å).
  Per film, only an OH step in E (start, end, centre E0, width) plus an offset per scan is fitted to all of that film's
  CV curves at once (Ru+Ti, the CTR quantity).
- 7 nm: E0 = 0.25 +- 0.02 V vs Ag/AgCl, width 0.15 +- 0.04 V. The size of the OH change is poorly determined
  (start and end each +-0.5); the position is well determined.
- 4.1 nm: the flat CVs allow an OH change of at most 0.03 anywhere in the window.
- Both films are fitted with the same structure source and the same assumption, so the comparison is like for like.
- Speaker note: letting the bridging O change too does not move E0 (0.22 +- 0.04 V).

  - Say plainly: 0% is the start of the window, not a measured 'all water' state. The 7 nm CTRs show the change has
    already begun slightly by 0 V.
- 7 nm (and 3.3 nm): two surface states, switched reversibly at about 0.2-0.25 V vs Ag/AgCl (about 0.5 V RHE).
- 4.1 nm:
  - the CTR rods do not change from -0.238 to 0.7 V (slide 8), and the CVs do not change from 0 to 0.7 V (slide 6);

  Confounded: the rest potential rises with beam exposure (logbook), and the argon purge (logged for day 3 only) and
  history differ by day.
- In the CTR model that state is the one with the higher OH fraction, under both bridging-O assumptions. That is as far as the
  data go. The amount of OH is not claimed.

SLIDE 13  Open questions and next steps
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Why the 4.1 nm film:
  - Strain alone does not fit: 3.3 nm is at least as strained and switches.
  - Beam dose before the CVs is ruled out (fresh spot). A blocking adsorbate (sulfate) or preparation remain open.
  - The work function (Jeong et al. 2026, backup B5) is context, not an explanation yet.
- What would test it (no beamtime follows; listed for collaborators and future work):
  - the current of the 4.1 nm film under the same program (lost on day 4);
  - a second 4.1 nm sample, CV first;
  - a potential staircase UP AND DOWN on a fresh spot with an intact window: the steady-state onset, separated from drift
    (the day-2 staircases are inconclusive, backup B7);
  - holds of 10 min at 0.7 V and at 0 V straight after a sweep, counting at one point, plus a rate series (1, 5, 25 mV/s):
    any slow process acting during holds and its time constant;
  - a wider window on 4.1 nm;
  - HClO4 instead of H2SO4;
  - CTRs on 3.3 nm;
  - a CV on an even rod of 4.1 nm.

SLIDE 14  Summary
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The 3.3 and 7 nm surfaces switch reversibly near 0.2-0.25 V vs Ag/AgCl. The 4.1 nm surface does not switch between
  -0.24 and 0.7 V: at the potential applied, its response is at most 1-12% of theirs at the same reflections.
- Assumption, stated: the 4.1 nm film was under potential control on day 4 (current record lost; slide 6, B8).
- The CTRs agree three ways:
  - the rods themselves;
  - fits under Rao's and an alternative bridging-O assumption, giving the same film difference;
  - the CTR-fitted states, used unchanged, reproducing the 7 nm CVs.
- The 4.1 nm surface sits in the 7 nm high-potential state throughout.

B3  How Rao et al. 2017 is used (figure: slideB3_bridging_O_free.png)
- What it is: in situ CTR of a RuO2(110) single crystal in 0.1 M HClO4 at 0.5, 1.0, 1.3, 1.5 V vs RHE. Water on the CUS
  sites at 0.5 V, half water / half OH at 1.0 V, OH at 1.3 V; the bridging O protonated (higher) at low potential.
- Why we need it: on our rods the OH fraction and the bridging-O height trade off, so one must be fixed (fig_variants.png,
  right panel). Rao's bridging-O heights are the external reference for fixing it: 0.35 Å below 0.6 V RHE, 0.2 Å at
  0.6-0.9 V, 0 Å above. Converted to our scale with the logbook's -0.238 V Ag/AgCl = 0 V RHE: 0.35 Å up to 0.35 V, 0 Å at 0.7 V.
- Where it enters:
  1. The CTR OH fits (slide 9): one of two assumptions. The other is the bridging O at 0 Å.
  2. The CTR-fitted states used unchanged to predict the CVs (slide 10).
  3. The joint CV fit (slide 11): film structure from the CTRs, bridging O held at Rao's 0.35 Å.
  4. A preset of start values (presets/fit/rao2017_RuO2_110_acid_low_potential.json).
- What it is NOT used for: no absolute OH coverage is claimed from it, and the film comparison does not depend on it (same
  result with 0 Å).
- What our data say back: the flat 4.1 nm CVs rule out Rao's potential-dependent bridging-O change on that film.
- Caveat: a single crystal in perchlorate, not a strained film in sulfuric acid. That is why it is one assumption of two.

B4  Why no single-point ratio from the CTRs
- I(3.16)/I(2.75) relative to 0 V depends on the chosen L window and a +-0.003 L alignment shift (10-90% range 0.8 to 1.4).
  The whole-rod comparison (slide 8) is used instead.

B5  Work function context (Jeong et al., Nat. Commun. 17, 2516 (2026); jeong_work_function.jpg)
- Figure: slideB5_work_function.png.
- Different samples from ours, so our films are placed by thickness only:
  - 3.3 nm: 4.73 (nearest point) or 4.84 eV (interpolated);
  - 4.1 nm: 4.86 or 4.85 eV;
  - 7 nm: 4.44 eV.
- Between 3 and 4 nm the curve moves by up to 1 eV in 0.6 nm, so a thickness-scale offset dominates.
- What can be said:
  - Both measurements show something abrupt between about 3.2 and 4 nm.
  - 3.3 and 7 nm switch at the same potential although their work functions differ by 0.3-0.4 eV.
- [NEEDED] KPFM on the beamtime samples, to make this a sample-by-sample comparison.

B7  The potential staircases on 3.3 nm (224, 225): inconclusive, not used
- Figure: slideB7_staircases.png.
- Program: 0 to 0.7 V in 0.05 V steps, 90 s each, one way, after CV 223. Recorded after the window film broke
  (during 153-221), with a bubble approaching ("Stopping day 2 here").
- 225 (0 -1 2.75): falls almost linearly by about 3.5% per step to -35% at 0.55 V, then flat. The CVs instead show a step
  centred near 0.25 V.
- 224 (0 -1 3.16): flat within +-2% at every step, although the CVs at this reflection (222, 223, just before) give +13-15%.
- A one-way staircase cannot separate potential from drift, and the two disagree with each other and with the CVs.
  Possible causes:
  - a bubble or lost electrolyte over the illuminated spot (no local potential control while EC-Lab runs normally);
  - the program not applied: ruled out, the EC-Lab files of 224 and 225 show a charging spike at every step;
  - a response at 3.16 that needs cycling.
- Why it does not touch the main result:
  - The CVs lock onto the potential cycle.
  - The 3.3 nm CVs before the staircases (222, 223) respond.
  - The 4.1 nm flat result rests on two spots (the CTR spot and the fresh CV spot), with no window or bubble problem logged
    on day 4.
- It shows that the X-ray signal at the illuminated spot can disagree with the logged program even when the program ran.

B8  Day 4 (4.1 nm): the current record, and why potential control is assumed
- Figures: ec_figures/ec01_voltammograms.png, ec04_potential_steps.png. Data: plot_data/12_ec/.
- Every day-4 file shows zero current within noise:
  - capacitance from the CVs -0.05 ± 0.05 uF, against 85-230 uF on 3.3 and 7 nm;
  - holds at 0 and 0.7 V differ by 0.1 ± 0.5 nA (3.3 nm: about 8 uA);
  - no charging at potential steps.
- The current channel was live (random noise 0.1-0.25 uA, the same current range as the other days).
- The facts behind the assumption are listed at the top. Under them, the charge must have flowed and was not recorded.
  Under 0.02% of the expected current reached the record, which needs a near-direct path around the ammeter. It was not
  found, and the equipment cannot be checked now.

B9  The currents of 3.3 and 7 nm, and the beam
- A steady anodic current flows on days 2 and 3:
  - 3.3 nm: about 1 mC per CV cycle, +3.7 uA at a 0.65 V hold;
  - 7 nm: up to tens of uA at 0.35-0.7 V, spiky in 311, 314 and 344.
  RuO2 alone should be close to reversible in this window.
- Not produced by the beam at the moment: during the 3.3 nm hold at 0.65 V, cutting the beam to 3.6% (scans 207, 208)
  left the current at 2.8-3.0 uA, against 3.7 ± 1.6 uA at full beam; the gaps between scans give the same (beam_current.py).
- Not excluded: radiolysis products built up over hours (H2O2), or an exposed contact.
- Effect on the results: the potential was applied on those days (charging at every step). iR at the switch potential,
  where the current is a few uA, is under 1 mV.

B10  The 3.3 nm (0 -1) rod at 0.65 V (153-221; fig_rod153.png)
- Phi rocking scans, L 0.30-3.70 (SPEC). The signal stays in the ROI on almost all frames; it is weak at low L.
- The rod scans match CV 222 at the same L and orientation matrix. CVs 120 and 125 (older matrix) are 2-3x weaker than
  the rod: probably measured off the rod maximum.
- Film-model fits stay poor (reduced chi2 48-80, minima from 4 to 29 TL), so the rod does not pin the 3.3 nm structure.
  The CTR states predict the 3.3 nm CV signs at 2.75 and 3.16 for every structure tried; the 3.25 sign only for a few
  sharp-fringe structures.
- The logbook says the cell window broke during this rod.

B6  Measurement notes
- The 7 nm (2 0) and (0 2) CTRs at 0 and 0.7 V used a 0.6 degree incidence angle (others 0.5). Each rod's own scale absorbs
  the footprint change, leaving under 1%; these rods are not used for the surface comparison.
- 4.1 nm scans 496 and 497 (same reflection, back to back) differ 5x in counts (filter or count time). Relative changes are
  unaffected.
- A polar distortion through the whole film (Jeong et al.) is not seen in our CTR fits: 4.1 nm 0.00 +- 0.045 Å (polar_test.py).


=============================================================================================================
LIKELY QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
=============================================================================================================
Q: Was the potential applied during the 4.1 nm CVs?
A: We assume so, and say so. The current record of that day shows no current at all, so it cannot confirm control.
   What supports control: Ewe followed the program; the rest potential was real and responded to the beam (an open lead
   reads about 10 V); CE and RE were in the same bubble-free reservoir; every electrode pair read a few hundred ohms through
   the cell. Under these, the film's potential had to follow, and the current must have gone unrecorded. The cause was not
   found, and the setup cannot be checked now (B8).

Q: If the potential was not applied, what is left?
A: The 3.3 and 7 nm results are unaffected: same switch potential, reversible, surface-specific. The 4.1 nm data become
   a no-potential control.

Q: Is the switch driven by the beam?
A: No sign of it. The response is locked to the potential, reverses with it, and changes sign with L. The anodic current
   on 3.3 nm did not drop when the beam was cut to 3.6% (B9).

Q: Why do 3.3 and 7 nm move in opposite directions at L 3.25?
A: Not interpreted. The 3.3 nm CV there (125) was measured off the rod maximum with an older orientation matrix, and 3.25
   sits on the steepest part of the 3.3 nm rod (B10).

Q: How much OH is on the 4.1 nm surface?
A: Not claimed. The absolute value depends on the bridging-O assumption; the film difference does not (slide 9).

Q: Your CTR fit has red. chi2 about 15. Why trust it?
   (Also: the worst-fitting rods, 7 nm (2 0) and (0 2), are the ones the logbook flags: "CTR did not stay in ROI".)
A: The main result does not rest on the fit. The CVs and the rod-to-rod comparison are model-free (slides 6, 8). The fit is
   used only for a comparison under the same model, and its states reproduce the 7 nm CVs (slide 10).

Q: Rao's values are for a single crystal in HClO4 vs RHE. Why use them?
A: As one of two assumptions, converted to Ag/AgCl. The relative result is the same with the bridging O at 0 Å. The flat
   4.1 nm CVs also argue against Rao's bridging-O change on that film.

Q: Could the 4.1 nm points be insensitive?
A: No. Four different L values all stay flat, and the model gives 14-25% sensitivity at each for any plausible thickness.

Q: Is it a thickness effect?
A: Not shown. One sample per thickness, and 3.3 nm switches. We claim that this film differs.

Q: Beam damage or drift?
A: The CV response is locked to the potential and changes sign with L; the 4.1 nm drift (+3% over 200 images) is common to
   both ROIs. The 4.1 nm CVs were on a fresh spot. For the CTR rods, the 7 nm change returns when the potential returns,
   and the 4.1 nm series jumps up and down in potential with no change (slide 8). Beam damage was noted on 3.3 nm on day 1;
   that sample's CVs were on day 2 after the cell was rebuilt.

Q: Does the change only happen while sweeping? (Could a dynamic process drive it?)
A: Not a simple one:
   - The anodic and cathodic sweeps overlap (no hysteresis at 5 mV/s), so the surface follows E within about 10 s.
   - The amplitude is the same at 25 mV/s as at 5 mV/s (scan 222: 17% vs 15% swing). A process that lags would shrink at the
     faster rate; one driven by the current would grow about 5x and flip sign between sweeps.
   - On 7 nm the long holds (CTR rods) do change, reversibly. The CTR-fitted states account for 60-100% of the CV change up to
     0.35 V and about half at 0.7 V (model-based, wide errors).
   Open points:
   - 147 (L 1.25) lags by about 80 s.
   - On 3.3 nm at 3.16 the signal returned to the hold level after cycling (223 -> 224), and the staircases disagree. These
     are day-2 data recorded after the window broke (B7).
   A slow process acting during holds (anion adsorption, beam-driven local chemistry, slow restructuring) is not excluded.
   Direct test: hold at 0.7 V and at 0 V after a sweep while counting, plus a rate series (1, 5, 25 mV/s).
   Film-spacing "breathing" (proton insertion) is ruled out: on 7 nm it moves 2.75, 3.16 and 3.25 the same way, and on
   3.3 nm the flat (-2 0 1.75) control limits it to at most about 0.15%.

Q: Sulfate?
A: Possible for the 4.1 nm film; HClO4 is on the next-steps slide.
