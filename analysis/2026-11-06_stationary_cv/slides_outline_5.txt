FIVE-SLIDE OVERVIEW: in situ SXRD of RuO2(110) films (3.3 / 4.1 / 7 nm) under potential control
=================================================================================================
Potentials vs Ag/AgCl; RHE = Ag/AgCl + 0.238 V (logbook). Figures named below are in analysis/2026-11-06_stationary_cv/
(slide_figures/, ec_figures/, onepager_fig.png). Numbers come from the saved results of this analysis.

Suggested title: "Potential-driven surface switching on epitaxial RuO2(110) films: two films switch alike, one does not"


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 1  Question, approach, answer
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: does the surface of epitaxial RuO2(110) on TiO2(110) respond to potential the same way in films of different
  thickness and strain?
- Samples: 3.3, 4.1 and 7 nm films, 0.1 M H2SO4, NSLS-II (Oct 2026).
- Measurements:
  - Stationary CVs: the X-ray intensity at a fixed reflection, recorded while the potential cycles 0 to 0.7 V at 5 mV/s.
  - CTR rods at fixed potentials.
- Why these reflections: on the (0 ±1) rods the metal scattering cancels, so the oxygen and adsorbate layers dominate.
  A surface change has a fingerprint: L 2.75 falls while L 3.16 rises. Beam or absorption artefacts move all
  reflections the same way.
- Answer up front:
  - 3.3 and 7 nm switch reversibly between two surface states, at the same potential: about 0.22-0.25 V (0.46-0.49 V
    RHE).
  - 4.1 nm does not switch anywhere from -0.24 to 0.7 V.
- Figure: slide04_reflections.png (the model change along (0 1), with the CV reflections marked).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 2  A reversible surface switch on 3.3 and 7 nm, at the same potential
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: onepager_fig.png:
  - (a) the change from 0 to 0.7 V, one point per CV scan;
  - (b) the switch completed vs potential.
  Relabel the 4.1 nm points to match slide 4.
- Method: each X-ray point gets the potential EC-Lab applied at that moment (SPEC and EC-Lab clocks agree within 1-2 s).
  Nothing is fitted to the timing.
- Change from 0 to 0.7 V:

  | Reflection  | 3.3 nm       | 7 nm          |
  |-------------|--------------|---------------|
  | (0 ±1 2.75) | -9.7 ± 1.3%  | -20.3 ± 0.7%  |
  | (0 ±1 3.16) | +9.3 ± 0.5%  | +14.9 ± 0.9%  |
  | (0 ±1 3.25) | -8.5 ± 0.8%  | +8.3 ± 0.8%   |

- It is a real surface change:
  - the fingerprint (2.75 down, 3.16 up) appears in both films;
  - OH-blind control reflections stay at their noise (7 nm (2 0 1.75) 0.2%; 3.3 nm controls 1.1-3.5%, noise 2.4-4.0%);
  - the sweeps overlap (no hysteresis at 5 mV/s), and the swing is the same at 25 mV/s;
  - it reproduces: 7 nm (0 1 2.75) four times, 3.3 nm (0 -1 3.16) twice.
- Same switch potential: half-way at 0.25 V (3.3 nm; scans 0.20-0.31) and 0.22 V (7 nm; scans 0.17-0.24).
  - This holds although 7 nm is partly relaxed (out-of-plane strain about 0.5%) and 3.3 nm is expected to be fully
    strained.
  - On Jeong et al.'s films (different samples), the work function differs by 0.3-0.4 eV over this thickness range.
- Speaker note: 3.3 and 7 nm move in opposite directions at 3.25. Not interpreted: CV 125 was measured off the rod
  maximum, and 3.25 sits on the steepest part of the 3.3 nm rod.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 3  The CTRs see the same change (7 nm)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figure: slide09_oh_fraction.png, panels a and b.
- On the slide:
  - The 7 nm rods change with potential and come back (0.7 vs -0.1 V: twice the repeat difference).
  - Fitted to the CTRs, the OH fraction rises from -0.1 to 0.35 V under both bridging-O assumptions, Rao's included.
  - These fitted states predict the sign and onset of the 7 nm CV changes, with nothing adjusted.
  - Absolute coverage is not claimed; the rise holds under both assumptions.
- Speaker notes:
  - rods: rms of ln(ratio) 0.077 / 0.080 against 0.039 / 0.040 for repeats;
  - at 0.15 V: 2.75 predicted -7 to -11%, measured -9%; 3.16 predicted +7 to +10%, measured +6.5%. Not reproduced:
    3.25, and the size at 0.7 V;
  - the 0.7 V drop under Rao is the imposed bridging-O change, not a measurement;
  - the fits share surface parameters with 4.1 nm; a 7 nm-only refit is pending.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 4  4.1 nm: no switch under applied potential
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Figures: slide06b_cv_response_synced.png (4.1 nm next to 3.3 and 7 nm); ec_figures/ec06_lab_wiring_tests.png.
- On the slide:
  - 4.1 nm stays flat at every reflection and in the CTRs: at most 1-12% of the other films' response.
  - The day-4 current record is empty, but the potential record and the rest potential were normal.
  - On the bench, only a WE also tied to ground reproduces day 4. The cell stays polarized and the current bypasses the
    ammeter, even through 100 Ohm.
  - Caveat: the beamline contact itself cannot be checked.
- Speaker notes:
  - bounds at 2.75 / 3.16: at most 3% / 12% of 3.3 nm and 1% / 8% of 7 nm (2 sigma); CTRs at six potentials agree to
    the repeat level;
  - support for control: OCV stable at 0.37-0.40 V and beam-responsive; CE and RE in the same bubble-free reservoir; all
    electrode pairs a few hundred ohms;
  - a disconnected WE also runs with no current, but its OCV drifts;
  - the beamline unit's ~1 uA lowest range explains the noise on the zero current;
  - swapped WE / CE leads are excluded by the absence of any capacitive current;
  - if control is not accepted, 4.1 nm is a negative control, and slides 2-3 stand either way.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 5  Summary, limits, next steps
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Summary:
  1. 3.3 and 7 nm surfaces switch reversibly between two states at the same potential (about 0.22-0.25 V, 0.46-0.49 V
     RHE), at the reflections the model marks as surface-sensitive.
  2. The 7 nm CTRs change with potential and return. States fitted to them predict the sign and onset of the CV
     changes.
  3. The switch potential does not follow strain or work function between 3.3 and 7 nm.
  4. 4.1 nm does not switch under applied potential (control supported, current record lost).
- Limits:
  - one sample per thickness, so no thickness trend is claimed (3.3 and 7 switch, 4.1 does not);
  - no absolute OH coverage, and no mechanism;
  - the 3.3 nm strain is not measured in this dataset;
  - the 3.25 sign difference is open;
  - a steady anodic current on days 2-3, not driven by the beam at the moment, has an unknown source.
- What would make it a paper:
  - a theory estimate of how strain should shift OH adsorption, to turn the equal switch potentials into a constraint;
  - lab CVs of all three films;
  - a second 4.1 nm sample;
  - a 7 nm-only CTR refit.
