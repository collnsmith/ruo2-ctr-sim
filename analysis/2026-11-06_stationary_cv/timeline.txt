WHAT THE ORDER CHANGES
  - 4.1 nm: the OCV rod came last and on a different spot. The "first polarization" idea is dropped, and the OCV rod
    is not used for comparisons.
  - 4.1 nm: the controlled potentials jump up and down in time (0, -0.1, 0.15, 0.7, -0.238, 0.35 V). The flat result is
    not hidden by drift.
  - 4.1 nm: the CVs were on a fresh spot, so dose from the CTR series is not the cause.
  - 7 nm: the 0.7 and -0.1 V rods were measured twice. They agree with themselves (rms 0.039-0.040) and differ from each
    other (0.077-0.078) in both directions, so the change follows the potential.
  - 7 nm: the OCV rod (first, after the CVs) matches 0 V (0.020); its rest potential was near 0 V (about -20 mV).
  - The 7 nm (2 0) and (0 2) rods left the ROI. This explains their poor fits.
  - RHE scale: +0.238 V (logbook), not +0.26 V. 0.35 V is 0.59 V RHE, just below Rao's 0.6 V boundary.
